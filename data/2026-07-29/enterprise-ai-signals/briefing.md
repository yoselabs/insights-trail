### 4. [new] Workforce AI Ultimatum — Two Major Surveys Quantify the Forcing Function

Two high-sample-size surveys published since the prior briefing converge on an organization-level transformation gap with sharp workforce consequences.

- **60% of companies plan to lay off employees who won't adopt AI** — a definitive policy signal, not a vague threat

## Cross-Source Patterns

### Pattern 1: The Governance Gap Is the Production Gap

Appearing across: Databricks (N=20,000+), Deloitte (N=3,235), Futurum/ServiceNow (live enterprise data), Domino (N=639), HN discussions.

Organizations with formal AI governance push **12× more projects to production** (Databricks). ServiceNow's AI Control Tower (500+ customers) is now a *buying trigger*, not an afterthought. Deloitte finds only 21% of companies have mature agent governance even as 85% plan to deploy agents. The Domino survey pegs the governance multiplier at 3.9× ROI. Forrester attributes 41% of agent pilot failures to unclear success criteria. HN user avgDev: *"I would not sleep well not knowing what is in production."*

The consistent signal: governance is the single most leveraged variable in whether AI investments pay off.

### Pattern 2: Spending Up, Benefit Claims Hollow — The Survey Paradox

Appearing across: PwC (N=4,454), Writer (N=2,400), Kyndryl (N=1,100), DoiT (N=500), Domino (N=639).

Every large-sample survey shows a consistent split: 86-97% of executives report increasing AI budgets or deploying agents; 12-29% report measurable financial impact. The gap is not closing — Domino explicitly notes a "two-year plateau." Yet 60% of companies (Writer) are now threatening workforce consequences for non-adopters, suggesting organizations are accelerating coercive adoption even without ROI proof. HN user vanuatu: *"Real ROI requires AI as a product component, autonomous metric improvements, outcome-based pricing — not 'Copilot'-style features."*

### Pattern 3: Enterprise AI Deployment Is a People Problem, Not a Technology Problem

Appearing across: PYMNTS (N=$1B+ companies), Deloitte (N=3,235), MIT NANDA (300 public projects), Kyndryl (N=1,100).

**71%** of executives at billion-dollar+ companies cite organizational readiness as the primary barrier; only **11%** cite technology. MIT's NANDA study of 300 public AI projects found 95% produced no measurable P&L impact — *"the problem was not the models, but how they were put into use."* This is the structural rationale for the $8B+ FDE race (OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS, Microsoft all committed in 6 weeks). Kyndryl: 57% have AI in core processes but only 23% are workforce-ready.

### Pattern 4: Vertical Specialization Outperforms General AI at Enterprise Scale

Appearing across: Fireworks AI ($1.5B, 95%+ specialized models), Harvey ($11B, legal), Hebbia (top 20 investment banks), Glean ($300M ARR, enterprise search), ServiceNow (AI ACV $1B, workflow-specific).

Every application-layer AI company with disclosed metrics is built on specialization, not general capability. Fireworks AI reports 95%+ of its 40T daily tokens come from specialized fine-tuned models. Harvey's $11B valuation is explicitly for legal-domain agents. The pattern is consistent: general AI becomes the infrastructure layer; revenue accrues to the vertical specialized layer on top.
