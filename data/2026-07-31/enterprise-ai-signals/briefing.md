### 4. [new] D&B AI Momentum Survey (N=10,000, 32 Countries): 76% Report ROI, Only 6% Data-Ready

**First seen 2026-07-31.** Dun & Bradstreet's quarterly global tracker of 10,000 businesses across 32 countries (Q3 2026, published July 28) is the largest enterprise AI deployment survey available with hard numbers.

Key data: 76%+ report measurable ROI — but this is much weaker than it sounds. Only 28% report "broad or strong ROI across multiple initiatives"; 48% see "pockets of ROI" from pilots. The critical chokepoint: only 6% say their enterprise data is "fully ready" to support AI at scale. 47% describe data as partially ready; 36% mostly ready. In other words, 94% of enterprises lack the data foundation required to scale AI beyond pilots — and this is the root cause of the production gap (only 34% are scaling into production) that shows up across every survey in this space.

### 8. [new] Gartner: $234B in Enterprise SaaS Exposed to Agentic Arbitrage Through 2030

**First seen 2026-07-31** (Gartner release July 1, 2026). Gartner estimates $234B of enterprise application software spend — approximately 20% of SaaS spending — is at risk from "agentic arbitrage" through 2030. The mechanism: AI agents complete tasks across multiple enterprise systems, reducing user interaction with traditional interfaces, breaking the link between user seat counts and vendor revenue.

Gartner Managing VP George Brocklehurst: "Agentic AI changes the economics of software. Agentic systems deliver outcomes directly, bypassing traditional UX-heavy applications and making the software invisible. This breaks the link between user growth and revenue growth for many enterprise software vendors."

Implications for enterprise buyers: this is a leverage point. Procurement teams that adopt AI agents for cross-system workflows gain negotiating power against per-seat SaaS vendors. Implications for vendors: service providers that can demonstrate outcome-based delivery (like the HCLTech deal above) are positioned to capture spend that flows away from dashboard-heavy SaaS.

Sources: [Gartner press release](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-07-01-gartner-says-us-dollars-234-billion-in-enterprise-application-software-spend-is-at-risk-from-agentic-artificial-intelligence) · [CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/agentic-ai-disrupt-234-billion-saas-spending/824530/) · [CIO.com](https://www.cio.com/article/4192242/agentic-ai-puts-234b-in-enterprise-saas-spending-at-risk-gartner-says.html) · [Channel Dive](https://www.channeldive.com/news/software-agentic-arbitrage-saaspocalypse-gartner/824309/)

## Cross-Source Patterns

### Pattern A: "Governance Multiplier" — Appearing Across Four Independent Data Sources

The single most consistent finding across this briefing: governance maturity is the strongest predictor of AI production success — and enterprises dramatically overestimate their own governance readiness.

- **Schellman N=525:** Mature governance → 78% agent production rate vs 22% for developing programs; yet 74% self-rate as audit-ready while only 27% are.
- **Domino N=639:** Governance multiplier 3.9× on ROI; 57% enterprise AI ROI failing to outpace spend.
- **Stanford N=51:** 61% had prior AI failure; governance (embedded, observability, leadership continuity) is the differentiating factor.
- **Deloitte N=3,235:** Only 21% have mature governance despite 34% claiming deep transformation.

The cross-source signal: organizations are deploying AI faster than they are building the governance to make it durable, and the firms with governance are separating from the pack on both agent deployment rates and ROI.

### Pattern B: Hyperscaler AI Revenue Acceleration Outrunning Analyst Models

All three major hyperscalers reported beats in their most recent quarters:
- **Azure +43%** (vs ~40% consensus); crossed $100B annual for first time
- **AWS +37%** (vs ~31% consensus); fastest growth in 18 quarters
- **Google Cloud +82% YoY** (reported Q2 2026 previously)

Gartner has revised its total IT spending forecast upward four consecutive times. The pattern is not noise — enterprise AI spending is structurally underestimated in advance and realized above forecast.

### Pattern C: Seat-Based SaaS Business Model Under Agentic Disruption

Appearing from multiple angles:
- **Gartner (July 1):** $234B enterprise application SaaS at risk from agentic arbitrage by 2030; seat-based pricing breaking down.
- **HCLTech $1.14B contract (July 3):** Client is buying outcomes (digital workplace + network ops running), not seats — AI executes.
- **Microsoft M365 price hike (+5-14%, July 1):** Incumbents raising prices to bundle AI before agents cannibalize the seat model.
- **OpenAI Presence, ServiceNow Control Tower, Salesforce Agentforce:** All structured as usage/outcome pricing, not seats.

The simultaneous Gartner warning and real-world contract evidence confirm that agentic AI is already beginning to substitute seat-based relationships, not just augment them.

### Pattern D: Data Readiness Is the Production Bottleneck — Not Model Capability

- **D&B N=10,000 (July 28):** Only 6% have enterprise data "fully ready" for AI at scale.
- **Deloitte N=3,235:** Only 25% moved 40%+ of pilots to production.
- **Forrester root-cause analysis (aggregated):** 41% of AI deployment failures tied to unclear success criteria; 33% to insufficient data access — none to model-quality problems.
- **Domino:** 57% ROI not outpacing spend despite 93% reporting improved production.

The failure mode is not model capability. Enterprise AI at scale is stalling on data preparation, governance, and workflow redesign. The 6% "fully data-ready" statistic (D&B, largest sample) is the single most policy-actionable number this cycle.
